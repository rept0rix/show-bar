Show Bar is a macOS app. It brings the Windows taskbar, window switching, snap, clipboard history, and copy-only screenshots to the Mac.

Install for Mac

Version 1.4. Open the disk image and drag Show Bar into Applications. macOS 14 or later. Not on the Mac App Store. This build opens on the Mac it was signed on.

macOS app by Naor Yanko · na0ryank0@gmail.com

The Windows taskbar

Hover the Dock. See the windows.

Point at an icon and that app’s windows open beside it, the way the Windows taskbar does.

  • The window under the pointer is marked, so you can tell it apart.
  • Click a picture to switch to that window.
  • X closes only that window. Minus minimizes it. Quit closes the app.
  • Rest on a card for two seconds to peek. Click to keep it, or move away to put it back.
  • Send a window to another desktop from its card.
  • While the preview is open, the Dock stays out. It hides again after you leave.

Command-Tab

The windows, not just the icons.

Mac Command-Tab shows apps. Show Bar shows the windows themselves, newest first.

  • Arrow keys move through the grid. Release Command to switch.
  • Every card stays on the screen.
  • Real app windows only. Password panels and system windows stay out.

Snap

Put the window where it belongs.

Hold Control and Option, then press a key. The front window takes that part of the screen. The same shortcut again puts it back.

  • Arrows: left, right, top, and bottom halves.
  • U, I, J, and K: the four corners.
  • Return: the whole screen.
  • The same choices are in the menu, under Snap window.

Clipboard

Keep what you copy.

Text, links, and pictures stay in a list. Control-Option-V opens it. A click pastes back into the app you were using.

  • Search the list. The menu also shows the last eight copies.
  • Copies marked as hidden passwords are skipped.
  • Up to 100 items, still there after Show Bar quits.

Screenshots

Copy the picture. Skip the file.

A screenshot goes straight to the clipboard. Nothing is saved to the Desktop. The preview stays in the bottom corner until you press X.

  • Command-Shift-3 copies the screen under the pointer.
  • Command-Shift-4: drag a region, or press Space and click a window.
  • Drag the preview into a chat, or paste with Command-V.

Everything in Show Bar

Show Bar runs on macOS. These are the Windows habits it adds, and the Mac details around them.

Windows, on the Dock

  • Hover a Dock icon to see that app’s windows.
  • Mark the window under the pointer.
  • Click to switch. X, minus, or Quit from the card.
  • Peek after two seconds, then click or leave.
  • Move one window to another desktop.
  • Choose the preview size, how many windows, and whether names show.

Switch and snap

  • Command-Tab shows windows, newest first.
  • Arrows move. Release Command to switch.
  • The grid fits the screen.
  • Control-Option arrows snap to a half.
  • U, I, J, K snap to a corner. Return fills the screen.
  • The same shortcut restores the old place.

Copy and capture

  • Clipboard history for text, links, and pictures.
  • Control-Option-V opens it. Search, then click to paste.
  • Hidden passwords are not kept.
  • Screenshots copy and do not save a file.
  • The preview stays in the corner until its X.

Lives on the Mac

  • Move the Dock to the left, the right, or the bottom.
  • Opens when the Mac starts.
  • The menu-bar icon stays when Settings is closed.
  • About shows the version, these notes, and Naor Yanko’s LinkedIn.
  • A blue dot and a notification when a newer version is ready.
  • Update from the menu. The new app must be signed as Show Bar.
  • After an update, Show Bar asks for a rating.
  • Admin shows how many times the install disk was downloaded.
  • Remove Show Bar from the app. It moves to the Trash.
